Design and Implementation of a Load Cell-Based Weight Scale

  • Arduino
  • Load Cell
  • HX711
  • Servo Motor
  • Sensor Integration
  • Calibration

An automated weight-sorting machine that reduces the need for manual product sorting, a process that is prone to human error and delays. The system is designed to sort items weighing up to 1 kg, such as fruit or pre-packaged goods.

How It Works

  • A load cell (a strain-gauge sensor — a thin metal bar that flexes very slightly under weight, changing its electrical resistance in proportion to the force applied) converts the weight of an object into a small electrical signal.
  • The HX711, a 24-bit analog-to-digital amplifier board, reads that tiny signal from the load cell and converts it into a digital value the microcontroller can understand, communicating over a simple 2-wire interface.
  • An Arduino (Uno Maker board) runs the program that reads the digital weight value from the HX711 and decides how to control the servo motor based on it.
  • A servo motor physically sorts the item into the correct bin based on which weight range it falls into.

Wiring

The load cell's four signal wires were soldered directly onto the HX711 module:

  • Red wire → E+
  • Black wire → E-
  • White wire → A-
  • Green wire → A+

Problems & Solutions

  • Calibrating the load cell — Borrowed access to the university's lab to compare readings against a reference scale accurate to 4 decimal places.
  • The 360° servo could only be controlled by direction, not by a specific angle — Switched to a 180° servo instead, which lets the program command it to hold an exact position rather than just spinning one way or the other.
  • The load cell arrived without any mounting bracket and couldn't be used as-is — Designed and built a custom bracket to hold it securely and support the applied weight correctly.
  • The load cell was overly sensitive to small changes in applied force — Required careful, patient calibration to get stable, repeatable readings.

Improvements Made

  • Redesigned the structure near the servo to better support the item's weight.
  • Programmed the two servos to work in closer coordination with each other.
  • Made the program wait for the weight reading to settle into a stable value before triggering the next action, instead of reacting to noisy, fluctuating signals.
