Servo-Controlled Two-Legged Walking Robot

  • Arduino
  • Servo Motor
  • Bluetooth
  • MIT App Inventor
  • Robotics
  • Embedded Systems

A project for the course ENGMC105 (Project-Based Learning for Mechatronics Engineering). A two-legged robot that walks using servo motors, controlled wirelessly over Bluetooth from a custom mobile app built with MIT App Inventor (a browser-based tool for building simple Android apps by dragging and connecting visual blocks, rather than writing code by hand).

Mechanical Design

Each leg uses three servo motors (a small motor that can be commanded to hold a specific rotation angle, unlike a regular motor that only spins continuously), each with a distinct job:

  • Hip joint — a 55g servo (stall torque 8.5–10 kgf·cm, meaning it can hold up to roughly 8.5–10 kg of force at 1 cm from its shaft before stalling) rotates to lift the foot forward for each step.
  • Knee joint — a smaller MG90S servo (13.4g) connects the foot to the hip and adjusts the foot's angle so the sole stays flat against the ground.
  • Foot/ankle joint — an MG955 servo tilts the body to maintain balance while the opposite foot is lifted off the ground.

In total, 6 servos (3 per leg) connect to an Arduino Uno, powered by a 9V battery.

Control Logic

The Arduino runs a main loop that listens for commands sent over Bluetooth from the mobile app and calls the matching function:

  • "FW" → moveForward() — moves several servos together in a two-step sequence for a smooth forward step.
  • "BW" → moveBackward() — the same motion sequence as walking forward, but reversed.
  • "Reset" → STOP() — returns every servo to its default starting angle.

Problems & Solutions

  • The knee joint moved unpredictably — the small servos, mounted side-by-side, weren't strong enough to properly support the leg while kicking a foot forward.
  • The knee joint couldn't bear the weight of the electronics box, breaking repeatedly during testing — solved by reducing the weight of the box.
  • The robot tipped over and couldn't lift its foot — caused by errors in the balance calculations; solved by making the box smaller and lighter.
  • The foot-mounted servo bore the robot's entire weight, occasionally becoming overloaded and slipping out of position during a step.

Improvements Made

  • Redesigned the structure near the servos to distribute the robot's weight more evenly.
  • Rewrote the movement program so pairs of servos work in closer coordination with each other.
  • Added a short pause to let force readings settle to a stable value before starting each new movement step, instead of reacting instantly to noisy sensor data.

Testing

The robot's walking gait and the mobile control app were tested together and recorded on video with a stopwatch, to evaluate how smoothly it walked and how well it responded to commands sent from the app.
